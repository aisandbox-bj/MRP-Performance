MRP Performance

How the whole replenishment process behaves — MRP response, the internal, supplier and 3PL legs, order sizing and stock outcomes — across every material in your SAP extracts, shown as distributions you can drill into.

1 · What it's for

Calibre Tune and Trace start from one material and ask whether it is healthy. This app starts from the process and asks where it breaks, how often and by how much — and for which materials. It uses the same SAP extracts and the same data engine as Tune, so a lead time here ties to the one Trace shows for the same order.

It answers questions like: how often MRP raises PRs and whether it goes quiet while parts wait · how fast a PR follows stock crossing Min · how many PRs are really cancelled · how long approval, buyers, suppliers and the 3PL take · whether V1 parts are ordered up to Max or topped up in small amounts · where stock arrives when it isn't needed or after the part has run out · how actual lead times compare with SAP's plan.

2 · Getting data in

Open Intake. The assessment type is already set to Supply-chain performance. Upload three files (Excel or CSV):

FileMust includeTip
MB51Posting Date, Material, Movement Type, Quantity, Purchase orderAll movement types for the plant; 12+ months. Value, entry date and storage location are used when present.
Inventory MasterMaterial, Unrestricted stock, MRP Type, Reorder Point, Maximum Stock Level, Safety Stock, Mfg Name, Moving priceSet the extract date (under the upload) — the stock rebuild starts from it. A date in the file name is picked up automatically.
PR HistoryPurchase Requisition, Item, Material, Requisition date, Release Date, Purchase order, Purchase Order Date, Changed On, Deletion Indicator, Processing status, Creation indicator, Quantity requested, Delivery DateDelivery Date is the need-by date — it powers the plan-vs-actual view.

The data-quality gate then shows what it found — including how many PR lines carry a need-by date and how many POs find a receipt in MB51. Name the dataset, Save (kept in this browser) or Download .json (a file you keep), then Open in Workbench.

A dataset downloaded from Calibre Tune also opens here (Dashboard → Open .json). Tune trims its downloads to that assessment's materials, so the review covers only those — the header says “Tune dataset”.

3 · Segments & the time window

Everything in the Workbench is scoped by the panel at the top.

Time window

All · 24 m · 12 m · 6 m · 3 m · Custom. Each leg is placed in time by its own starting event: PR → PO by the month the PR was created, supplier by the month the PO was placed, 3PL by the month goods reached the 3PL, stock events by the day they happened.

Segment tiles

Drag a dimension from the palette onto the tile area (or just click it). Tiles combine with AND. Click a tile's header to open or close its settings; ✕ removes it.

  • Number tiles (movement, consumption events, consumption value, unit cost, stock value, stock on hand, material lead time, PR lines) have three modes: Quartile — tick Q1–Q4; the tile shows each quartile's cut points, and they're cut across all materials so they don't move as you add tiles · Top / bottom % · Range (min / max).
  • Pick-list tiles (manufacturer, MRP type, material group, purchasing group, stock status) — search, tick values, choose Include or Exclude.
  • Material / text — number or description contains.
  • PR trigger — MRP-created, manual or unknown; applies to PR lines, not to stock events.

Examples: PR → PO on the top-moving quarter of parts = Movement → Q4, then Internal. 3PL performance on items over $10,000 = Unit cost → Range, min 10000, then 3PL. One supplier's lead time = Manufacturer → pick it, then Supplier. Clicking a row in any breakdown table adds that value as a tile.

4 · Reading a distribution

  • Closed — the leg finished. Still open — it hasn't yet; drawn lighter, at its age so far (“at least this long”). They are never dropped: in recent months the slow ones are exactly the ones still open.
  • Out of sequence (amber, far left, “!”) — the end of the leg is dated before its start, e.g. a PO dated before its PR. Listed, never rounded to zero.
  • P10 / median / P90 count still-open items. A “≥” means open items sit at or below that point, so the true value is at least that. The closed-only figure is shown underneath.
  • By month — one box per month: box = middle half, whisker = P10–P90, white tick = median of closed items; the caret marks the P90 once open items are counted. The % under each month is how many have closed; months under 90% are dimmed (provisional). Click a month to focus the view on it.
  • Breakdown — every manufacturer / MRP type / group side by side with its spread. Sort any column.
  • Drill — click any bar, or use Fast tail / Slow tail / Still open / Out of sequence, to list the orders behind it. The list opens in a panel docked to the bottom of the screen, over the page — drag its top edge to make it taller, ✕ or Esc closes it. Sort, and export to CSV. ▦ Table swaps a chart for its numbers in the same box (▦ Chart swaps back).

Nothing moves when you click. Buttons, pickers and tabs change what a card shows, never where things sit: the line just under each card title says what is showing right now (e.g. “Showing POs raised per week, split by Outcome”, or the focused month), tables that change with a picker keep one height and scroll inside, and pop-ups — the Filters card, drill lists, chain details, the Intake's column mapping — open over the page. The only deliberate jumps: choosing a view in the left rail, and clicking a row in Trigger crossings or Order to Max on the deep-dive, which zooms the timeline and scrolls up to it.

5b · Material deep-dive

Click any row in a drill table, the Materials table or the heat map to open that material. ‹ Prev / Next › steps through the list you came from, in the order it was sorted; ← Back to Workbench returns you to the same view, segment and window. The Material box (top right) jumps to any material by number or description.

The page is a stack of blocks you tailor in the View bar: tick blocks on or off, drag them into any order, or pick a preset (Mechanics · Graphs · Everything). Your layout is remembered.

  • Time-axis blocks (cyan edge) that sit next to each other draw on one time scale: stock vs Min / Max / SS · MRP cadence · PR / PO / receipt events · consumption · procurement chains. Put the cadence under the stock graph to see stock cross Min (▼) and how long the system took to raise a PR, a PO and a receipt. Hover anywhere for that day; click a chain bar (or a dot in Consolidated durations, or a PR line in Raw data) for its dates — they open in a panel docked bottom-right; use 12 m / 6 m / 3 m or click a crossing to zoom.
  • Stock graph: cyan = rebuilt stock on hand; violet = stock + what's on order (open POs, goods at the 3PL); red shading = stocked out; amber shading = below the trigger line with nothing on order.
  • Annual progression: per year, each leg's median end to end — how approval, buyer, supplier and 3PL moved year on year.
  • Month-on-month band: pick a leg; median (white), mean (violet), P25–P75 and P10–P90 per month or quarter.
  • Raw data: the PR lines, the MB51 ledger (each movement's effect on site stock and the rebuilt stock at the end of each day) and receipts by PO.

Multi-material views in the Workbench

  • PR / PO volumes & MRP cadence — PRs (by the day they were created — with no MRP run log, that date is the evidence of a run) or POs per day / week / month, split MRP vs manual, V1 vs PD, or outcome; trigger debt underneath on the same axis.
  • MRP activity by material — materials × weeks. Amber = days below the line with nothing on order; cyan dot = MRP PR; violet diamond = manual PR. An MRP gap is an amber run without dots.
  • Materials in the segment — every material's parameters in one sortable table.
  • Month-on-month bands on every leg replace the monthly boxes: read the median and mean for direction, the band width for predictability.

6 · Settings & KPI targets

⚙ Settings & targets (top right of the Workbench):

  • MRP churn window (default 2 days) · provisional until % closed (default 90%) · stale PO after (default 365 days).
  • KPI targets in days for each leg — leave them blank until you've seen the baseline. Once set, charts draw the target and each view shows the share within target.

7 · Definitions & assumptions

  • 107 = goods arrived at the 3PL; 109 = received at site; first date per PO + material.
  • Stock is rebuilt day by day from MB51, walking back from the Inventory Master stock on its extract date, with the same movement-type rules as Tune (site stock only).
  • Trigger line = Min for V1, safety stock for PD. PD without safety stock is driven by demand (reservations), which these files don't carry, so its zero-stock days are counted as stockouts but not as trigger failures. Same for V1 with Min 0.
  • Cover = an open PO or goods at the 3PL. A PR on its own is not cover.
  • Cancelled = deletion flag + processing status N. MRP churn = MRP-created, never a PO, cancelled within the churn window of creation (Changed On stands in for the cancel date).
  • Creation indicator: B = MRP, R = manual, blank = unknown.
  • Manufacturer stands in for vendor. Order qty = PR qty (no PO data yet).
  • Min / Max / SS / MRP type are today's values. Changes during the window can't be seen.

8 · Where your data lives

Everything runs in your browser. Saved datasets live in this browser's storage under this app's own name (mrpPerf), separate from Calibre Tune — clearing one never touches the other. Nothing is sent anywhere. The GitHub repository holds the app's code only; SAP extracts and datasets are never committed to it.
